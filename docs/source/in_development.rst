Models under development
=========================

The features on this page are in the code but are not described in the
software paper. Their input keys, outputs and interfaces may change between
versions without notice. Case paths are relative to ``z3st/cases/``.

Cohesive phase-field fracture
-----------------------------

``z3st/models/cohesive_model.py`` (:class:`~z3st.models.cohesive_model.CohesiveModel`)
implements the variational phase-field model of cohesive fracture of
Vicentini, Heinzmann, Carrara and De Lorenzis, *J. Mech. Phys. Solids* 207
(2026) 106424, doi:10.1016/j.jmps.2025.106424. Its variational form is adapted
from the authors' MIT-licensed reference implementation
(https://github.com/jonas-heinzmann/phase_field_cohesive_fracture), as stated
in the module header and in ``NOTICE``.

It differs from the brittle model of
:class:`~z3st.models.damage_model.DamageModel`: the elastic energy is neither
degraded nor split. A reversible eigenstrain :math:`\eta` is a primary
unknown, and the degradation acts on the strength potential
:math:`\pi_0(\eta)`:

.. math::

   W = \psi_e(\varepsilon - \eta) + a(\alpha)\,\pi_0(\eta)
       + \frac{G_c}{c_w}\left(\frac{w(\alpha)}{\ell} + \ell\,|\nabla\alpha|^2\right)

Only the AT2 form is implemented, :math:`a(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)^2`,
:math:`w(\alpha) = \alpha^2`, :math:`c_w = 2`. The strength surface is set by
the material keys ``p_c`` (critical pressure) and ``tau_c`` (shear strength),
and ``ell`` is not a strength calibration parameter. In 2D and 3D the
eigenstrain enters through :math:`\mathrm{tr}\,\eta` and
:math:`\|\mathrm{dev}\,\eta\|`, each one DG0 unknown, with the strength-surface
norm selected by ``r_norm`` (``"1"``, ``"2"`` or ``"inf"``). In 1D a scalar
eigenstrain is used and ``p_c`` plays the role of :math:`\sigma_c`. The phase
field is stored in the damage field ``D``.

Configuration, in ``input.yaml``:

.. code-block:: yaml

   models:
     mechanical: true
     cohesive:
       ell: 5.0e-5          # regularisation length (m), required
       r_norm: "2"          # "1", "2" or "inf"; ignored in 1D
       eigenstrain_band: [0.995e-3, 1.005e-3]   # optional

Material card keys: ``Gc``, ``p_c``, ``tau_c`` (``tau_c`` is unused in 1D).

Cases:

- ``verification/cohesive/bar_1D`` (in the local suite). A 1D bar in tension
  with :math:`E = 10` GPa, :math:`G_c = 1` J/m\ :sup:`2`,
  :math:`p_c = 5` MPa and :math:`\ell = 50` µm, with the eigenstrain confined
  to the central element. ``non-regression.py`` checks each computed
  equilibrium state against the closed-form localised solution of the paper
  (Eq. 85, with the phase field at the centre read from the stress through
  Eq. 69). The relative error of the softening branch is 1.5e-3 in the gold,
  with a tolerance of 2e-2.
- ``verification/cohesive/strength_surface_2D``,
  ``strength_surface_r1_2D`` and ``strength_surface_rinf_2D``. These
  directories hold no tracked input files or results, and are not cases.

Automated identification of material laws
-----------------------------------------

``verification/fuel/creep_law_discovery`` selects a creep mechanism from a
library of candidate laws (:math:`S, S^2, S^3, S^5, \sinh S` with
:math:`S = \sigma/\sigma_\mathrm{ref}`) using noisy stress-relaxation data
generated by Z3ST itself. ``discover.py`` integrates the relaxation ODE at a
material point by backward Euler, computes the parameter sensitivities by
forward-mode automatic differentiation with dual numbers implemented in the
script, fits by Gauss-Newton and removes terms by their share of the creep
strain, choosing the final model by the one-standard-error rule. The method
follows the idea of EUCLID (Flaschel et al., 2022) and is implemented
independently of the EUCLID codes.

Scope: the identification acts on a scalar ODE at a material point, outside
the finite-element solver. It is not an adjoint of the finite-element problem,
and it is not available as a model or an input key. The stored result
(``output/discovery.json``) selects the cubic Norton term alone, with a
coefficient within 1.63 % of the value used to generate the data. The case is
excluded from the local suite (501 steps).

Planned: a comparison of the crystal-plasticity case
``verification/plasticity/crystal_single_grain`` with the same law written in
MFront and called through MGIS, on the same single-grain problem.

``z3st/conference/fenics2026/demo/identify_creep.py`` is the earlier
demonstration of the same approach: it identifies the two parameters
:math:`(A, n)` of a known Norton law.
